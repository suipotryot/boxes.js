Holes & Notches

Two different ways to cut extra openings into a piece.

Holes

A hole is a rectangle, with optional rounded corners, that can sit anywhere inside a piece — a wall, the base plate, the lid, or a drawer panel. A piece can have several holes. Typical uses are a cable pass-through or a mounting hole for a fixing.

Select a piece, open its Holes section in the inspector, and add a hole as one comma-separated line:

FieldMeaning
Position XDistance from the piece's left edge, in mm.
Position YDistance from the piece's top edge, in mm.
WidthHole width, in mm.
HeightHole height, in mm.
RadiusCorner rounding, in mm — 0 for sharp corners. Capped at half the smaller of width/height, since a bigger radius would make the corner arcs overlap.

For example, 20, 10, 30, 15, 3 places a 30×15 mm hole with 3 mm rounded corners, 20 mm from the left and 10 mm from the top.

Grip notches

A grip notch is a different kind of cutout: it's always anchored to a wall's own free top edge, rather than floating freely like a hole. It's typically used to make a finger-pull opening — for example on the open side of a drawer.

Grip notches use the same compact comma-separated row editor as holes, with width, depth, radius, and offset along the edge.

Tip

Both editors validate your values and offer an Auto-fix button when a hole or notch doesn't fit the piece — for example when it would run past an edge.
