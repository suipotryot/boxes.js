Walls & Joints

Thickness, height, and how pieces lock together at the corners.

Thickness

Set separately for the two kinds of walls described in The Grid Model:

  • Outer thickness — the perimeter walls and the base plate.
  • Inner thickness — the dividers between cells.

Use the inner thickness that matches whatever sheet material you're actually cutting the dividers from — it doesn't have to match the outer thickness.

Height

  • Outer height — the perimeter wall height.
  • Default inner height — applied to every divider, overridable per segment (see The Grid Model).

Finger joints

Pieces that meet at a corner or a T-junction are locked together with finger joints (interlocking teeth), controlled by four fields:

FieldWhat it does
Finger widthThe width of each tooth of the joint.
Space widthThe width of each gap between two teeth.
Min. marginThe flat margin left at each end of a row of teeth, before the teeth start.
PlayEach tooth is shrunk and each gap is widened by this amount, so the pieces fit together without forcing once cut — it compensates for laser kerf and material tolerance.

Kerf / burn compensation

The burn (kerf) setting is the width of material the laser removes at the cut line. The app slightly enlarges outlines and shrinks holes by this amount, so pieces measure the correct size once they've actually been cut.

Tip

If pieces come out slightly undersized or oversized after cutting, adjust burn rather than the piece dimensions themselves — it's meant to be tuned per material and per laser.
